"Read it in…" panel's WHOSE VOICE row, about x787–1200, y343–440 of `docs/vision/reference/renders/s07.png`, inside

## What was built

### `src/components/player/known-voices.ts` (new, pure, relative imports only)
- `knownVoices(sources, max = 4)`: the names this device knows, from the shelf the Read Room already read:
  - every `metadata.narrator` of every tape on the shelf, own tapes and Sent-to-you references alike;
  - every `signature.voiceConsent[].voice` of your **own** tapes, reduced to its lead name by T03's
    `narratorFromByline` ("Mia, age 4 — with Mom" → "Mia"). A sent reference keeps no consent records (T20b).
  - Order: most recent first, by when the tape was saved (`createdAt`, own) or reached this shelf (`addedAt`,
    sent); ties keep the shelf's order. Within one tape, the narrator comes before its consent names.
  - Names that differ only in case are one voice; the most recent spelling stays. At most four.
  - The consent fallback `"narrator"` (T04b's `UNNAMED_VOICE`, a recorded voice nobody named) and blank names are
    not names.
- `voiceRow(known, reader)`: the names the row shows. Today's reader of the target is always among them: when the
  four known names leave it out, it is added first and the row is cut back to four.
- `voiceName(raw)`: a name as the Creator takes it: trimmed, whitespace collapsed, at most 40 characters (code
  points; `VOICE_NAME_MAX` is T03's `NARRATOR_MAX_CHARS`), `undefined` when empty or not a string.
- `voiceFromHash(hash)`: the name a door URL carries in its fragment (`#voice=<name>`).
- `sameVoice(a, b)`, and the copy constants `BROWSER_VOICE` ("Browser voice"), `SOMEONE_NEW` ("Someone new"),
  `READING_NOW` ("READING NOW"), `KNOWN_VOICES_MAX`, `VOICE_PARAM`.
- `VoiceSource` is `{ kind: "own", entry: { createdAt, manifest } } | { kind: "sent", tape: { addedAt, narrator? } }`,
  so the Read Room's own shelf items are passed as they are.

### `src/components/player/read-it-in.ts` (modified)
- `readerOf(target)`: who reads the target today.
  - `{ kind: "browser" }` when no page carries `text.audioUrl` (the house tape), or for a sent reference without a
    narrator.
  - `{ kind: "voice", name }` for `metadata.narrator` (or the sent reference's narrator).
  - `{ kind: "unnamed" }` for a recorded tape that names nobody (no cassette says READING NOW).
- `creatorUrl(target, mood, voice?)`: T22a's URL, plus `#voice=<name>` (URI-encoded) when a name is chosen. The
  browser voice, or an empty "Someone new" field, appends nothing. See Deviation 1 for why it is the fragment.

    short of it; `kind="browser"`: no dot (nobody recorded it); `kind="new"`: lucide `Plus` in tape-red where the

### `src/components/player/ReadItIn.tsx` + `read-it-in.css` (modified, T22a's files)
- Between the "read you this one" line and "WHAT SHOULD IT FEEL LIKE?":
  - `[data-rin="voice-label"]`, the mono 10.5px label "Whose voice?" (upper case by CSS: "WHOSE VOICE?");
  - `[data-rin="voices"]`, `role="radiogroup"` named by that label: `VoiceCassette kind="browser"` "Browser voice",
    one cassette per `voiceRow(knownVoices(shelf), reader)` name, then `kind="new"` "Someone new";
  - "Browser voice" is checked by default. A chosen name whose tape leaves the shelf falls back to it. The choice is
    the panel's, kept when the target changes (the voice is who will read, not a fact of the tape).
  - READING NOW goes on "Browser voice" when `readerOf` says browser, else on the cassette of the narrator's name.
  - Choosing "Someone new" (click, tap or arrow) shows `[data-rin="voice-field"]`, a text field labelled "Whose voice?"
    (`aria-labelledby` the same label), `maxlength=40`, and moves focus into it. It is a paper label to write on:
    2px ink border, radius 8, Fraunces italic 16px (no zoom on iOS), 44px tall, placeholder "Their name".
- The row: `display: grid; grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fill, minmax(68px, 1fr)); gap: 10px 5px`. The cassettes
  take as many columns as the row holds and share what is left, so a full row spreads across the panel as s07's
  does: five in the 370px panel (about 7px apart), six from 1280px (438px), four on a 375px phone. A longer row wraps.

### `src/components/player/ReadRoom.tsx` (modified)

### Creator: `types.ts`, `draft.ts`, `Studio.tsx`, `ToolPanels.tsx` (modified)
- `StudioState.voiceName?: string`, draft-only (documented in `types.ts`). `blankState` leaves it out.
- `loadDraft` keeps it through `voiceName()` (tidy, ≤ 40) and drops anything else in its place.
- `Studio.tsx`:
  - `takeVoiceName()` runs in T15's after-mount boot effect, beside `takeOpenTapeQuery()`. It reads `#voice=` (and a
    hand-typed `?voice=`) and cleans both off the address with `history.replaceState`. The name is used only with
    `?open=`: it rides with the tape it was chosen for.
  - `PendingOpen` carries `voice`, so the "Open <title>?" dialog's Open applies it too.
  - `applyOpenedTape(tape, mood, voice)` sets `voiceName` with `remix`. Opening a file through "Open a tape" passes
    no voice, so another tape clears it. Magic, which sets `remix: null`, clears it too. "Start a new story" and
    "delete everything" start from `blankState()`.
  - The gate's byline default: for a re-voiced tape, `readerRef.current ?? state.voiceName ?? ""`. `readerRef` is now
    `string | null`, null until the gate is passed for this story (set back to null by `replaceStory`), so the
    grown-up's own typing still wins, including clearing it. Without `voiceName` the default is "" exactly as T15 left
    it.
- `ToolPanels.tsx`: `VoicePanel` takes `readingAs?: string`. While no take runs, the subtitle is "Reading as <name>";
  recording keeps "Listening… tap again when you are done"; without a name, "Tap the red button and tell this page".
- Publish: unchanged. The gate's byline "Mom" goes through T03's `resolveNarrator` and T04b's `recordedVoices`, so the
  copy gets `metadata.narrator` "Mom", one `voiceConsent` record for "Mom", and keeps the house tape's author (T15).
  No change to `narrator.ts`, `consent.ts` or `ParentGate.tsx`.

## Deviations from the reference and the ticket

1. **The voice rides in the URL fragment (`#voice=<name>`), not the query (`&voice=<name>`).** The ticket's own
   criterion "names are read from this device only and never sent anywhere" cannot hold with a query: a Next client
   navigation fetches the route's RSC payload **with the query**. Probed on the running dev server with
   `router.push('/studio?open=house&mood=Hush&voice=Mom')`: the first request was
   `fetch http://localhost:3000/studio?open=house&mood=Hush&voice=Mom&_rsc=yudn9`, so the name reached the server
   (and would reach its logs). A fragment is never part of a request. The Creator reads it in T15's after-mount
   effect and cleans it off with the rest, as the ticket asks; it also accepts and cleans a hand-typed `?voice=`.
   Test 3 proves no request carried the name. T18 made the same call for the reply seed (sessionStorage); the
   fragment keeps the ticket's shape (the name on the door URL, read and cleaned by the Creator).
2. **No locked "MADE VOICE · PHASE 3" cassette** (Phase 3; the ticket says so).
3. **"Mom" appears only when this device knows it or it is typed.** The reference's Mom, "Mia, age 4" and Grandma
   Rose are illustrative; here the row shows the shelf's own narrators and consent names by their lead name
   ("Mia", not "Mia, age 4").
4. **Checked and READING NOW are two things.** In s07 they coincide on "Browser voice". Here the label-yellow ring
   marks the checked cassette (the voice the copy will be read in) and the turning reels plus the caption mark who
   reads the target today. With the house tape and nothing chosen, both are on "Browser voice", as in s07.
5. **Row spacing.** s07 spreads five cassettes with `space-between` (8px apart). Here the cassettes take
   `auto-fill` columns of at least 68px, 5px apart, sharing the leftover: five fit the 370px panel about 7px apart
   (a full row looks like s07's); fewer stand at the left at the same spacing instead of spreading wide; six fit
   from 1280px; more wrap. The caption is 10.5px (the panel's mono size; s07 draws 11px) so it runs only 6px past
   its cassette.
6. **"Someone new" and its field are new UI** (s07 has neither): a cassette with a tape-red plus where the record
   dot goes, and a paper field (placeholder "Their name") under the row while it is checked.
7. **The panel is taller than s07's and its door is below the fold at 1140×712.** T22a's panel was 583px (s07: 604px)
   and ended at y710; the row adds 111px (label 14 + 13, gap 8, cassettes 60, caption 17), so the panel is 694px and
   "Make our own copy" ends at y≈803 at 1140×712 (the room scrolls; at 1280×720 the panel is 648px). The row itself
   is in the first screen (the fidelity test asserts its bottom ≤ 712). The extra height comes from T22a's own
   choices (the mono target line, the wrapped line at 370px, 48px chips with 8px gaps and two wrapped blurbs, the
   48px pill) and the room header (panel top 127 vs s07's 92), not from this row; changing those is T22a's call.

## Decision log entry

- **T22b · the chosen voice rides in the URL fragment.** "Read it in…" → "Make our own copy" appends the chosen name as
  `#voice=<name>` (not `&voice=`): a Next client navigation fetches the route with its query (`/studio?…&voice=Mom&_rsc=…`
  observed), and the ticket requires that names never leave the device; a fragment is never sent. The Creator reads
  `#voice=` (and a hand-typed `?voice=`) in T15's after-mount effect and cleans it off. Draft-only
  `StudioState.voiceName` prefills the gate's "read by"; the name reaches a manifest only as T03's narrator and T04b's
  consent voice.
- **T22b · known voices.** Narrators of every shelf tape (own and sent) and the lead names of your own tapes' consent
  voices, most recent first (own: saved; sent: arrived), case-insensitive dedupe keeping the most recent spelling, at
  most four; the consent fallback "narrator" is not a name; the target's own narrator is always in the row.
- **T22b · kit VoiceCassette** (brief LOCAL 6) built without its locked state; checked = label-yellow ring, reading
  now = slow reels + "READING NOW" caption (static under reduced motion).
